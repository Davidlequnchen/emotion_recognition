# Emotion Recognition Public Dataset Survey

English translation of [datasets_survey.md](datasets_survey.md) (Chinese); the two have the same content.

Survey date: 2026-10-23. Goal: survey public emotional speech datasets that can be used to evaluate emotion recognition
models (emotion2vec+) and audio large language models (audio LLMs).
Selection criteria: the same sentences spoken in different emotions, so the words give no clue; high-quality labels;
large size; and a clear licence and way to get the data.

Method: searched papers, official project pages, GitHub, Hugging Face, Zenodo, OSF and similar sources, and collected the
information on each dataset.
Sizes are in decimal GB, from the file listings of each platform's API or from HTTP requests; "about" marks an estimate,
and "not published" means the platform gives no size.
"Unverified" marks numbers that could not be confirmed from a primary source. ✅ marks the datasets downloaded locally
(see section 6); for these, the number of clips, the duration and the sampling rate were measured after downloading.

## 0. Key points

- **CREMA-D is in EmoBox**, so it is probably part of emotion2vec+'s training data, and emotion2vec+'s results on CREMA-D are optimistic (not a strict held-out result).
  Among the surveyed datasets, ESD, MEAD, RAVDESS, TESS, SAVEE, EmoDB, CASIA, Emozionalmente, SUBESCO, JL-Corpus, CaFE, EMOVO and EmoV-DB are in EmoBox too.
- **What is public about emotion2vec+'s training data:**
  - seed was fine-tuned on the academic data of EmoBox (201 h). EmoBox has about 294 h in total, and the subsets used are not listed, so to be safe all of EmoBox is treated as data the model has seen.
  - base and large were fine-tuned on "filtered large-scale pseudo-labelled data" (4,788 h and 42,526 h). The authors say the data engineering details will be published later; the sources of the pseudo-labelled data are unknown, so no public dataset can be ruled out 100%.
  - emotion2vec's pre-training used IEMOCAP, MELD, CMU-MOSEI, MEAD and MSP-Podcast.
- **Best "clean" candidates (none in EmoBox)**: MCAE-SPPS (Mandarin, 2025/26), THAI-SER (Thai, the largest), the target sentences of MSP-IMPROV (English), nEMO (Polish, 2024).
- **English**: no dataset was found that is large, text-parallel, perceptually validated and not in EmoBox all at once; the only option is to combine MSP-IMPROV, MESS and Expresso.
- **Evidence for this approach**: [LISTEN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10444) (2025) tested audio LLMs on the text-neutral sentences of CREMA-D, ESD, TESS, SAVEE and RAVDESS.
  With audio only, accuracy was just 16.5–34.9% ([Qwen2.5-Omni](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20215) 34.0%, [Gemini 2.5 Pro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06261) 34.9%); with text only it was 85–97%.
  So these models rely mainly on "turning speech into text" rather than really listening to the voice.

**Why this ranking:**

1. **MCAE-SPPS**: a thoroughly parallel design, with perceptual labels from 40 listeners and intensity ratings for every clip; its 200 sentences have far more lexical variety than CREMA-D's 12;
   its 7 classes match those of emotion2vec+ exactly. Released only in 2025, and no record was found of any audio LLM trained or evaluated on it.
   Drawbacks: only 6 speakers; NC licence (no commercial use).
   Citation: Li M, Zhou A, Yan H, Li Q, Ma C, Wu C. *Scientific Data*, 2026, doi:10.1038/s41597-026-06976-z; data doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/9JYZC.
2. **THAI-SER**: the most speakers, crowd-sourced perceptual labels with agreement, two intensity levels, and the most permissive licence.
   Drawbacks: the scripted part has only 3 sentences; "frustrated" has no counterpart in emotion2vec+, so using only neutral, angry, happy and sad is recommended.
   [VoxEmo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08936) (2026) has already used it for zero-shot evaluation of [Qwen2-Audio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10759) and [AF3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08128) (evaluation only, no training). Citation: arXiv:2507.09618.
3. **MSP-IMPROV**: the most established English data with "controlled text + crowd-sourced perceptual labels + not in EmoBox".
   Drawbacks: the controlled-text part has only about 1.3k clips, an agreement must be signed, and [OSUM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13306) has used it as a test set. Citation: Busso et al., IEEE TAC 8(1):119–130, 2017.
4. **nEMO**: new data from 2024, fully parallel, direct download; its emotions are close to CREMA-D's (no disgust, but surprise).
   Drawbacks: small, recorded at home; VoxEmo has evaluated on it. Citation: Christop, LREC-COLING 2024, pp. 12111–12116.
5. **HCUDB**: 34 professional actors and 11,220 clips, with the same sentences recorded in many emotions; HCUDB1 has dimensional ratings, useful for intensity analysis.
   Drawbacks: mapping its 11 classes to emotion2vec+ needs compromises; the application process is in Japanese.
6. **MCAE pseudo-sentences**: word meaning is removed completely, so a model can only judge by the voice; the best test of "does the model really listen"; two intensity levels. Drawback: fewer clips than CREMA-D.
   Citation: Gong et al., *Behavior Research Methods* 55(3):1441–1459, 2023.
7. **MESS**: a small English supplement, parallel, CC BY licence. Drawbacks: "calm" instead of "neutral", only 4 classes; the public version has acted labels only.
   Citation: Morgan, JSLHR 62(11), 2019.
8. **MASC**: the only Mandarin data with "many speakers + large + parallel", useful to test generalisation across speakers. Drawbacks: 8 kHz, no listening test, paid.

## 4. Notes

- **Non-commercial or restrictive licences**: MCAE-SPPS (NC; the paper itself is NC-ND), nEMO (NC-SA), Expresso and EARS (NC), RAVDESS and CaFE (NC-SA),
  TESS (NC), ESD (research only), EmoV-DB (non-commercial), HCUDB and OGVC (research only), MSP-IMPROV (academic licence), MASC (paid LDC licence).
  Permissive licences: THAI-SER (BY-SA), MESS, Emozionalmente and SUBESCO (BY), CSEMOTIONS (Apache), Thorsten (CC0).
- **Registration or application required**: MSP-IMPROV (agreement), ESD (form + email), HCUDB and OGVC (NII-SRC, in Japanese), KESDy18 (ETRI), MASC (LDC), IITKGP, GEMEP,
  CAVES (form), MCAE pseudo-sentences (view-only link), IEEE DataPort (subscription).
- **Unofficial mirrors such as Kaggle** (EMOVO, SAVEE, CASIA): their content and licence have no official guarantee; where possible, check them against the original source before use.
- **Known label and audio problems**:
  - THAI-SER: the assigned and crowd-majority labels often disagree, so using only the subset with agreement ≥0.71 is recommended; some recordings come from Zoom or from untreated rooms; always use the same one of the 4 microphones.
  - MCAE-SPPS: the 43% threshold for keeping a clip is lenient; the classes are unbalanced after filtering (angry 1,129 clips, fearful 671, neutral and sad about 1,190).
    For strictly parallel comparisons, use the subset where the same speaker and sentence kept all 7 emotions. The loose per-emotion folders on OSF are incomplete; use the per-emotion zips.
  - MESS: the public Zenodo version has only 1,800 clips and acted labels; the listeners' ratings must be requested from the author.
  - nEMO: only 3 of the 9 speakers are professional voice actors; there is no predefined data split.
  - MSP-IMPROV: the target sentences are spoken inside improvised dialogues, so the perceived labels may differ from the intended emotion.
  - ESD: no listening test; 16 kHz. MEAD: only some sentences are parallel across emotions. MASC: 8 kHz, and the original files are lossy WMA. HCUDB2: no ratings.
- **Which data audio LLMs have used (only where a source says so)**:
  - [Kimi-Audio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18425)'s SFT used IEMOCAP, MELD, RAVDESS, SAVEE and ESD.
  - [OSUM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13306) trained on ESD, IEMOCAP, MSP-Podcast, MER2023, MELD and BIIC-Podcast, and tested on IEMOCAP, MER2023, M3ED, **MSP-IMPROV** and ESD.
  - [Audio Flamingo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01831) trained on MSP-Podcast, EmoV-DB, JL-Corpus, TESS, MELD and OMGEmotion; CREMA-D and RAVDESS were explicitly excluded from training and used only for zero-shot evaluation.
    [AF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03983) also evaluated zero-shot on CREMA-D and RAVDESS, and [AF3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08128) evaluated on IEMOCAP.
  - [SALMONN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13289): trained on IEMOCAP sessions 1–4 and tested on session 5.
  - [Qwen-Audio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07919): under 1k hours of emotion recognition training data, without names; EmoV-DB files appear in its SFT examples; evaluated on MELD.
  - [Qwen2-Audio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10759) and [Qwen2.5-Omni](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20215): only report evaluation on MELD; training data not published.
  - Gemini: training data not published; the third-party [LISTEN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10444) evaluated [Gemini 2.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06261) on CREMA-D, ESD, TESS, SAVEE and RAVDESS.
  - [VoxEmo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08936) (2026) evaluated Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct and AF3 zero-shot on 35 corpora, including THAI-SER, nEMO, ESD, MEAD and CREMA-D.
  - **No record was found of any audio LLM trained or evaluated on MCAE (SPPS and pseudo-sentences), HCUDB, MESS or MASC.**

## 6. Downloaded locally (2026-10-23; ESD on 2026-10-26; RAVDESS, TESS and JL-Corpus on 2026-10-27)

Set `download.dataset` in `config/config.yaml` to the dataset's name (`all` for all of them), then run `python scripts/common/download_data.py`. Every file is checked against the checksum its host publishes (except ESD and JL-Corpus, see the last column),
and running the same command again after an interruption resumes the download (ESD and JL-Corpus are single zips and start again from the beginning). The data stays in `data/` and is by default not in git (several licences forbid sharing copies); for a machine that cannot reach these hosts, MCAE-SPPS, nEMO, TESS and JL-Corpus are exceptions for now (see the README).

These nine datasets take about 30.5 GB locally (THAI-SER 17.3 GB, ESD 5.8 GB, JL-Corpus 2.2 GB). Zips are kept after unpacking, so MCAE-SPPS, MESS, ESD, RAVDESS and JL-Corpus take about twice their download size;
THAI-SER and nEMO are Parquet files (reading them needs `pyarrow`), and on its first run `scripts/evaluation/evaluate.py` extracts the audio it evaluates into each dataset's `audio/` folder (for THAI-SER the studio condenser microphone, or the Zoom recording for online sessions).
ESD is for research only: before using it, complete the licence form as described on its [GitHub page](https://github.com/HLTSingapore/Emotional-Speech-Data) and email it to the authors.
ESD and THAI-SER are both downloaded, but are currently left out of the evaluation by `evaluation.exclude` in `config/config.yaml`; their earlier reports are in `reports/archive/`.
